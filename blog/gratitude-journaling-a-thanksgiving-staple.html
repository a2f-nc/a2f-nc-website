Gratitude Journaling: A Thanksgiving Staple

Lillian Chu · Nov 23, 2026

Thanksgiving is this week! It’s a time for noticing and appreciating the people and things in your life that you’re grateful for. With a few days off from school there will be some good down time, not just for going into a food coma after eating your body weight in your favorite Thanksgiving food, but also for slowing down and reflecting. Reflection is a really important practice to ground yourself in reality so that you can be thankful for all that you have. I know it can seem intimidating or even kind of amorphous, so we wanted to give you some tools, a simple journaling template to articulate your gratitude. So get ready for some acronyms!

This is called the GIFT method. It’s a set of questions to go through, each with a purpose designed to lead you through a thought process of noticing, understanding, and applying an event in, or aspect of your life.

G - Grasp

What did I notice or observe?

  • What stood out or moved me?
  • What was unexpected or surprising?
  • What might I have missed if I wasn’t paying attention?

I - Insight

What relational or spiritual connection did I see?

  • Who reflected Christ to me and how?
  • What aspect of God’s heart or provision did I experience?

F - Formation

How is God forming me through this?

  • How is God using this to grow or shape me?
  • What have I learned about Jesus through this?
  • How did this expand my understanding and appreciation of the church/body of Christ?

T - Testify

How will I respond and bear witness?

  • How can I imitate what I’ve seen?
  • How can I pass on what I’ve received through their example?

You don’t have to answer every single question, these are just some examples to get the juices flowing. Choose one clear angle or focus. Specifically name the blessing. Highlight a turning point. Then connect it to a truth about God.

Gratitude journaling is something that has made a big difference to me personally. After I graduated from college I went overseas for a couple of months, then came home to a season of unemployment. Going from such an intense experience serving God to staring at LinkedIn on my laptop everyday was, needless to say, somewhat discouraging (see the 5/20/26 blog for details on that). But taking the time to not only think about the wonderful experiences I had had, but articulate them in a framework I could use to recognize God’s hand still at work in my life and apply those lessons to my character and walk with God…game changer.

So, here is a miniature GIFT example:

About a year ago, I hit a particularly rough patch in that hard season I mentioned earlier. I texted my long-distance best friends from college for a little support. What was unexpected or surprising? They dropped what they had going on in their lives and drove several hours to be with me. One of them bought me Christmas decorations. This allowed me to experience God’s provision not only through their generosity, but their sheer love and compassion. They reflected Christ in their tangible care for me as well as the way that they took every opportunity to verbally remind me of Jesus’ love. I learned that this is what the body of Christ looks like when lived out genuinely: showing up for each other, truly bearing one another’s burdens. Lastly, I can only hope to imitate the love shown to me by being that kind of friend to them too.

Maybe things are going smoothly and things to celebrate are coming easily to you. Or maybe life is tough right now and you’re having a hard time finding the silver lining. The truth is that we are all wired to look for things that are wrong or could be better and we tend to overlook the things that are good, especially the simple things.

Gratitude is a practice that takes practice. But the more you do it, the more you see things to be grateful for around you. So give this method a try this year! You might be surprised.
